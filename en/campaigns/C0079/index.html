Cultural Terror and the "Executed Renaissance" (1933–1938)

C0079 january 1933 – december 1938 Ukrainians

Actor: USSR Instruments: Secret Police and Security Services, Government, Propaganda

The abrupt rollback of the policy of "Ukrainization." The start of the physical extermination and political defamation of the intellectual elite - writers, scholars, film directors, as well as the old national communists (M. Skrypnyk was driven to suicide)[1]. In parallel with the physical terror came the destruction of the Ukrainian Autocephalous Orthodox Church, large-scale censorship, the rewriting of history, and the artificial convergence of the Ukrainian language with Russian; works published in the Ukrainian SSR before 1933, in the words of the compiler of the anthology "Executed Renaissance," were "destroyed as a result of Moscow's new course toward the rout and colonial provincialization of Ukraine"[2].

Procedures Carried Out7

T0004 Rewriting of History

Rewriting of History USSR Propaganda

The construction of a new Soviet-imperial historical canon to supplant the Ukrainian past: "from the late 1930s a thesis took hold in the historical scholarship of the USSR," proclaimed in the works of B. Grekov, K. Huslystyi, and F. Yastrebov, "of Kyivan Rus as the 'common cradle' of the history of the Ukrainian, Russian, and Belarusian peoples," and "in 1939 B. Grekov advanced the proposition of the ethnic unity of the 'Rus people' in the era of Kyivan Rus"[3]. This scheme displaced M. Hrushevsky's conception and denied the independence of Ukraine's history.

Procedure page →C0079.T0004

T0021 Neutralization of the Opposition

Neutralization of the Opposition USSR Secret Police and Security Services

Physical annihilation and isolation of the Ukrainian intelligentsia, writers, and scholars, as well as of the party opposition (national communists), in the course of targeted repressions. Fabrication of cases (including the "UNC" case) and driving representatives of the national elite to death, including the historian Mykhailo Hrushevsky, the writer Mykola Khvylovy, and People's Commissar Mykola Skrypnyk[1].

Procedure page →C0079.T0021

T0022 Terror

Terror USSR Secret Police and Security Services

Creation of an atmosphere of total paralyzing fear in society through the fabrication of cases (for example, the "Union for the Liberation of Ukraine") and the staging of public show trials[1].

Procedure page →C0079.T0022

T0045 Destruction of Historical Memory

Destruction of Historical Memory USSR Secret Police and Security Services

Institutional destruction of national historical scholarship, closure of research schools, and removal of the works of Ukrainian historians from scholarly circulation[1].

Procedure page →C0079.T0045

T0100 Seizure of Religious Institutions

Seizure of Religious Institutions USSR Secret Police and Security Services

Mass closure and destruction of churches, as well as repressions against clergy, aimed at the complete destruction of the Ukrainian Autocephalous Orthodox Church (UAOC)[1].

Procedure page →C0079.T0100

T0101 Censorship

Censorship USSR Government

Mass confiscation and destruction of literature declared "nationalist," and rigid control over the printed word and the arts[1].

Procedure page →C0079.T0101

T0113 Linguistic Assimilation

Linguistic Assimilation USSR Government

The forced orthography reform of 1933, aimed at artificially bringing Ukrainian grammar and terminology closer to the Russian language[1].
