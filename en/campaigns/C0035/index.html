Military Annexation of the Crimean Khanate (1783)

The seizure of the Crimean Khanate and its forcible incorporation into the Russian Empire: "WE have resolved to take under OUR dominion the Crimean peninsula, the island of Taman, and the entire Kuban side"[1]. With regard to Ukraine, the annexation was secured through the forced mobilization of Cossacks as a military resource, the colonization of the adjacent southern steppes to create a loyal rear, and the erasure of Cossack toponyms: the lands of the destroyed Sich were assigned by Catherine II's manifesto of 3 August 1775 "as convenient to the Novorossiysk Governorate"[2]. For Crimea this meant the liquidation of the state and its ruling dynasty: "in the spring of 1782 Khan Shahin Girey was forced to flee under Russian protection"[3], the manifesto on the taking of Crimea was signed by Catherine II on April 8, 1783 (PSZRI, coll. 1, vol. XXI, no. 15,108)[4], and the khan was exiled to Kaluga; the indigenous population was being squeezed out in waves of emigration - "if before 1787 only 8 thousand had emigrated, by 1792 it was already tens of thousands"[3].

The use of Ukrainian Cossacks as a military resource for storming Turkish fortresses and consolidating imperial claims to Crimea: at Ochakiv "the 'loyal Cossacks' took part in the storming of the fortress"[5], and in the storming of Izmail "in total over 6 thousand Black Sea Cossacks took part in the battle"[6].

The colonization of the steppes adjacent to Crimea with settlers to create a loyal rear: as early as 1752, New Serbia was formed from Serbs – "officially – to guard the southern frontiers from Tatar and Turkish raids, but in fact – to subdue the Zaporozhians"[7], and in 1776 Potemkin directly ordered "not to return fugitives"[8], accelerating the settlement of the south.

April 1783
T0043 Erasure and Renaming of Place Names
C0035 Military Annexation of the Crimean Khanate (1783)

S0008 Government

The erasure of the historical names of the Cossack southern territories used as a springboard for the annexation of Crimea: Catherine II's manifesto of 3 August 1775 extirpated "the very name of the Zaporozhian Cossacks" and poured their lands into an imperial construct – "assigning them as convenient to the Novorossiysk Governorate"[2].
