Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)

Open aggression and a large-scale military invasion of the territory of Ukraine by the Russian Federation from several directions: "On the same day, Russian troops crossed various border points into Ukraine, including through Belarus, and launched attacks by land, air and sea"[1]. The goal of the campaign is the complete destruction of Ukrainian statehood and the erasure of Ukrainian identity. The UN commission established that the violations "amount to war crimes and include wilful killings, attacks on civilians, unlawful confinement, torture, rape and the forced transfer and deportation of children," and that strikes were carried out "with an apparent disregard for civilian harm and suffering"[1]. The campaign is accompanied by the destruction of entire cities (urbicide), ecocide, and a systematic policy of assimilation in the occupied territories[2]. The OSCE mission found "clear patterns of IHL violations by the Russian forces in their conduct of hostilities": had they respected "their IHL obligations in terms of distinction, proportionality and precautions in attack," civilian casualties would have been far lower[3].

On February 24, 2022, Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, unleashing the largest and bloodiest war in Europe since World War II and sharply escalating the conflict that had begun back in 2014 with the annexation of Crimea and the war in Donbas. Vladimir Putin, president of the Russian Federation, announced a "special military operation," setting as its goal the "demilitarization and denazification" of Ukraine and effectively denying its statehood, after which Russian troops attacked from the north (from Belarus toward Kyiv), from the east, and from the south (from Crimea)[1]. After the failure of the attempts to seize Kyiv quickly and the retreat of Russian troops by April 2022, mass war crimes were uncovered, in particular the Bucha massacre[1]. In the fall of 2022, Ukraine carried out successful counteroffensives in the Kharkiv and Kherson regions, liberating Kherson, while Russia in response illegally annexed four partially occupied regions[4][5]. In 2023, the Ukrainian counteroffensive did not achieve significant results, and in 2024 Russia captured Avdiivka and began a slow advance in the east, while Ukraine carried out a daring incursion into the Kursk region; Russia retook the territories in the Kursk region with the help of North Korean troops fighting on its side[6][7]. As of May 2026, Russia occupies about 20% of Ukraine's territory[8], about 8 million Ukrainians have become internally displaced persons, and 6-7 million refugees are in EU countries[9]. The International Criminal Court has issued arrest warrants for Putin and five other Russian officials for war crimes, including the deportation of Ukrainian children, and the UN has documented systematic human rights violations, torture, sexual violence, and strikes on civilian targets[10][11][1]. Russia has also carried out massive missile and drone attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure, while Ukraine in response has attacked Russian oil refineries and conducted Operation Spiderweb, which struck strategic bombers deep inside Russian territory[12][13]. According to expert estimates, by the end of 2025 the number of killed and wounded on both sides had exceeded one million people, with 2025 becoming the deadliest year for Ukrainian civilians since the start of the invasion[14][15]. Peace negotiations have repeatedly broken down; after Donald Trump came to power in 2025, the United States sharply shifted its position toward Russia[16].

February 2022
T0019 Military Intervention
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0010 Regular Army

The invasion by the regular Russian army and massive missile and air strikes on Ukrainian cities: "On 24 February 2022, the President of the Russian Federation, Vladimir Putin, announced his decision to conduct a 'special military operation' in Ukraine to seek its 'demilitarization and denazification'. On the same day, Russian troops crossed various border points into Ukraine, including through Belarus, and launched attacks by land, air and sea"[1]. The UN Commission investigated the events of February-March 2022 in Kyiv, Chernihiv, Kharkiv and Sumy Provinces[1].

February 2022
T0104 Mass Killings of Civilians
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0010 Regular Army

The deliberate killing of civilians in occupied territories: the UN commission established "a widespread pattern of summary executions in areas that the armed forces of the Russian Federation controlled in 17 localities of Chernihiv, Kharkiv, Kyiv and Sumy Provinces, with the highest number in Kyiv Province, including in the city of Bucha," confirming "the execution of 65 men, 2 women and a 14-year-old boy"; "in more than half of the executions investigated, witnesses had last seen the victims in the custody of the armed forces of the Russian Federation"[1].

February 2022
T0105 Total Destruction of Infrastructure
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0010 Regular Army

The practice of "urbicide" - the deliberate erasure of entire Ukrainian cities (for example, Mariupol), the destruction of infrastructure, and the entombment of the dead in the foundations of ruins[2]. The blowing up of the Kakhovka dam on 6 June 2023: "the breach of the dam at the Kakhovska Hydroelectric Power Plant in June 2023 resulted in massive flooding downstream, triggering an environmental disaster that has already had catastrophic effects on communities across the region," while "the flooding killed 34 civilians (32 in Kherson region and 2 in Mykolaiv region)" on Ukrainian-controlled territory alone[17]. The scale of the disaster on the very first day: "about 16 thousand people on the right bank of Kherson region are in the critical zone," with 80 settlements under threat of flooding[18].

February 2022
T0118 Extermination on a Territorial Basis
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0010 Regular Army

Deliberate mass killings of people without regard to their ethnic origin, solely on the basis of their Ukrainian citizenship and affiliation with the Ukrainian state[2].

February 2022
T0137 Disinformation
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0021 Propaganda

Total information control and a ban on calling the invasion a war - officially it was declared a "special military operation" whose stated aims were "demilitarization and denazification"[1]. The informational cover for energy terror rested on the "response" narrative: "There is a lot of noise now about our strikes on the energy infrastructure of the neighboring country. Yes, we are doing this. But who started it?"[12].

February 2022
T0138 Nuclear Blackmail
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0009 Diplomacy

Nuclear blackmail and the armed seizure of nuclear facilities: the IAEA recorded that "a large number of Russian tanks and infantry 'broke through the block-post' to the town of Enerhodar, a few kilometres from the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant," while the Ukrainian regulator reported that "Russian infantry troops were moving directly towards the site of Ukraine's largest nuclear power plant," calling the situation "critical"[19]. International bodies also voiced concern about "the unacceptable nuclear rhetoric used by Russian officials"[1]. Already on February 24 the Chornobyl NPP was seized as well: Ukraine informed the IAEA that "Russian forces had taken control of the facilities of the State Specialized Enterprise Chornobyl NPP" within the Exclusion Zone[20].

February 2022
T0124 Deprivation of Electricity and Heating
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

Систематические кибератаки на энергетические компании и муниципальные теплоснабжающие предприятия в условиях полномасштабной войны для прекращения подачи электроэнергии и тепла украинским гражданским потребителям[21][22].

February 2022

C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation

Масштабные операции спецслужб РФ по кибершпионажу, перехвату данных (в том числе через взлом публичных сетей Wi-Fi) и скрытому развертыванию деструктивного ПО против украинских организаций и граждан[23][24].

February 2022
T0125 Forcible Removal of Children
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0008 Government

The mass deportation and forcible transfer of Ukrainian children: the International Criminal Court established responsibility for "the war crime of unlawful deportation of population (children) and that of unlawful transfer of population (children) from occupied areas of Ukraine to the Russian Federation"[10], issuing arrest warrants against Putin personally and against the commissioner for children's rights Maria Lvova-Belova. Research by the Yale lab documents that "Russia's federal government is operating a large scale, systematic network of camps and other facilities that has held at least 6,000 children from Ukraine within Russia-occupied Crimea and mainland Russia during the past year"[25].

February 2022
T0023 Filtration Camp System
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

The creation of a network of filtration camps in the occupied territories: "'filtration' is defined within the context of this report as the multi-step system Russia and its proxies have deployed in territory they currently occupy within Ukraine to register, interrogate, and, in some cases, indefinitely detain people residing there at the time Russia and its aligned forces took that locale under its control," and "there is evidence the system was created weeks before the invasion began and likely grew following Russia's capture of Mariupol in April 2022"[26].

February 2022
T0043 Erasure and Renaming of Place Names
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

Deliberate restoration of old Soviet or imperial names to captured cities and streets (in particular, the renaming of Bakhmut to Artemovsk) in order to erase Ukrainian national memory and visually integrate them into the space of the Russian Federation.

February 2022
T0035 Forced Passportization
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0024 Police Apparatus

Coercing residents of the occupied territories into taking Russian passports: "the policy of mass conferral of Russian citizenship to residents of occupied areas of Kherson, Zaporizhzhia, Donetsk, and Luhansk regions exacerbated the coercive environment there," and "those who refused Russian passports were denied access to public services, notably social security, healthcare, and education, and risked unemployment" and "faced a heightened risk of arbitrary detention when crossing checkpoints"[17].

February 2022
T0109 Sham Expression of Popular Will
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

The staging of sham "referendums" in the occupied territories in the autumn of 2022: the UN General Assembly noted that "the illegal so-called referendums were organized from 23 to 27 September 2022 in these regions as attempts to modify the internationally recognized borders of Ukraine," and determined that they "have no validity under international law and do not form the basis for any alteration of the status of these regions of Ukraine"[5].

February 2022
T0117.001 Migratory Replacement
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

Artificial alteration of the demographic composition of the population in the occupied territories (for example, in destroyed Mariupol) by incentivizing the relocation of citizens of the Russian Federation and their mass purchase of housing, against the backdrop of squeezing out the indigenous residents.

February 2022
T0052 Expropriation of Resources
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

The large-scale plunder of the economic resources of the occupied territories: "from 2022 to 2025, almost 15 million tonnes of Ukrainian grain, valued at 6.8 billion US dollars, were illegally removed from the temporarily occupied districts of Ukraine"[27]. Strikes on port infrastructure were accompanied by "the destruction of grain and other products destined for export"[17]. The plunder extended to industry: "the owners of the stolen metal - the Azovstal, MMKI and Zaporizhstal plants - have already filed a series of criminal complaints"[28].

February 2022
T0112 Theft of Cultural Property
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

The systematic plunder of historical and cultural heritage and the mass removal of museum collections: according to Ukraine's minister of culture, "one million seven hundred thousand items of our cultural heritage were stolen from the occupied territories – including archaeological artifacts and museum collections. The Russian Federation has unlawfully appropriated them, in clear violation of all norms of international law"[29]. The Council of Europe characterizes these attacks on cultural heritage as having a "genocidal dimension"[29].

February 2022
T0158 Territorial Marking with Imperial Symbols
C0103 Full-Scale Invasion (from February 24, 2022)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

From April 2022 the Russian occupation administrations marked the seized territories with imperial signs - in stone and on paper. In Kherson oblast more than 110 Lenin monuments had been dismantled before the invasion, with practically none left; after the occupation the invaders erected ten new ones: the first in Henichesk in April 2022 - the first Lenin monument on Ukrainian territory since 2014 - and at the end of that same month a monument was returned to its pedestal by the city council in Nova Kakhovka[30]. In Mariupol on 5 May 2022 a monument to a woman with a red flag was unveiled, and the ceremony was published in his Telegram channel by Andrei Turchak, secretary of the general council of "United Russia"; the Square of the Warrior-Liberators, named in honor of those who freed the city from the Nazis, was given back its Soviet name of "Lenin Komsomol Square" by the occupiers[31]. In parallel the occupation administration papered the territory with banners: in Kherson and Nova Kakhovka hung Russian-language billboards reading "Russia is here forever!," "Kherson - forever with Russia," "Kherson is a Russian city," and for them they bought, for twenty cents on a stock-photo site, a picture by the Ukrainian photographer Anna Pasichnyk - her son Yaroslav with a friend in vyshyvankas in a rapeseed field near Kyiv - painting a Russian flag in behind the children's backs[32]. In Balakliia in Kharkiv oblast the occupiers pasted over a city banner: first they hung "Ukraine and Russia are 'one'" on top of it, then a poster with a tricolor reading "We are one people with Russia!". On 10 September 2022 Ukrainian soldiers tore the tricolor down - and beneath it emerged the lines of Shevchenko that had stood there through the entire occupation: "Fight - and you shall prevail! God is helping you! Truth is with you, glory is with you, and holy freedom!"[33]. The signs held only until de-occupation: on 11 November 2022, a "Russia is here forever" poster in liberated Bilozerka in the Kherson region was torn down by local residents - the video was published by the Telegram channel "Nikolaevsky Vanek"[34].
