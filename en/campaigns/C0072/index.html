Creation of a Puppet Government and Disguising the Intervention (December 1917 – Early 1918)

Having suffered defeat in Kyiv, the pro-Bolshevik delegates moved to Kharkiv, where they held an alternative congress and created a puppet government - the People's Secretariat, "the first Soviet government of Ukraine," created "as an alternative to the Ukrainian Central Rada and the General Secretariat of the Ukrainian Central Rada"[1]. This served as political cover for the start of the First Soviet-Ukrainian War (1917–1918) and allowed Soviet Russia to disguise the direct invasion by its troops as an internal civil war[2]. During the offensive, the aggressor provoked an uprising at the Arsenal plant in Kyiv, while the invasion itself was accompanied by heavy fighting for junction stations and the Battle of Kruty (January 1918), where the defense of the state was taken up by youth[3]. In February, the Red troops under the command of M. Muravyov - "the initiator of mass executions in the city"[1] - carried out a massacre in captured Kyiv. The campaign was halted by the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (February 1918), which brought the UNR international recognition and forced the RSFSR troops to retreat[2]. Hrushevsky - an eyewitness and head of the Central Rada - describes the preparation from within: "In Kyiv itself, from all sides, left and right alike, fierce agitation was waged against the Ukrainian government, the Central Rada, and Ukrainians as such. It brought the local Ukrainian regiments to near-total disintegration"[4].

The holding of an alternative congress in Kharkiv and the creation of a Moscow-controlled "People's Secretariat" to imitate an internal struggle: it was created "as an alternative to the Ukrainian Central Rada and the General Secretariat of the Ukrainian Central Rada" and "acted in close contact with and under the direct leadership of the highest state and party institutions of Soviet Russia"[1].

December 1917
T0018 Proxy War

S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

The use of the Kharkiv government as formal cover for external aggression, to present the military invasion as a civil war inside Ukraine: the People's Secretariat "announced the overthrow of the UCR and the recognition of only its own decrees as valid. It introduced the decrees of the Council of People's Commissars of Russia in Ukraine"[1][2].

December 1917
T0020 Staging a Coup

G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)

The Bolsheviks' provocation of an uprising of the Arsenal plant workers in Kyiv itself at the very moment of the Red troops' offensive: it was "an armed action organized by the Kyiv committee of the Russian Social Democratic Workers' Party (Bolsheviks) against the Ukrainian Central Rada," and "it was planned for the moment of the approach of Soviet troops to Kyiv in the course of Soviet Russia's war against the UNR of 1917–18"[5].

December 1917
T0019 Military Intervention
C0072 Creation of a Puppet Government and Disguising the Intervention (December 1917 – Early 1918)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0010 Regular Army

The direct deployment of Red troops, heavy fighting for the stations, and the storming of Kyiv. The Battle of Kruty (January 1918) took place "during the offensive on the city of Kyiv by the troops of Bolshevik Russia": advancing against the cadets and the student company was "a 6-thousand-strong Bolshevik detachment of Petrograd and Moscow Red Guards," and the defenders' losses amounted to "up to 300 killed, wounded, captured"[3]. The march on Kyiv was led by M. Muravyov: he "headed the march of Soviet troops on Kyiv in January 1918"[1]. Overall command was exercised by V. Antonov-Ovseenko: "in December 1917 - March 1918 - commissar for combating counter-revolution in the South, he led the Soviet troops in the struggle against otaman A. Kaledin and the Ukrainian Central Rada"[6].

December 1917
T0104 Mass Killings of Civilians

The Muravyov massacre of February 1918 after the capture of Kyiv by the Red troops: the EIU characterizes Muravyov as "the initiator of mass executions in the city," and later he was himself arrested on the charge of "'condoning mass executions and lootings that led to the fall of Soviet power in Ukraine'"[1]. Hrushevsky on the campaign's commander: "the chief commandant, the former gendarme Muravyov, who in February had boasted of executions of Ukrainians and the ruin of their property"[4].
