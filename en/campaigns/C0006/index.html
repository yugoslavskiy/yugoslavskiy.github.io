Campaigns

Publication of the "Synopsis" (1674)

C0006 january 1674 Ukrainians

Actor: Tsardom of Muscovy Instrument: Moscow Patriarchate

The creation of the first large-scale ideological justification for the seizure of lands by the Muscovite tsars – the "Synopsis, or a Brief Collection from Various Chroniclers on the Origin of the Slaveno-Rossian People," published by the printing house of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra in 1674[1]. The author's calculation was political: "to give the Muscovite tsar the motivation to continue the struggle with the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth for the liberation from Catholic rule of the remaining part of the 'single Orthodox people'"[2].

Procedures Carried Out4

T0004 Rewriting of History

Rewriting of History Tsardom of Muscovy Moscow Patriarchate

For the ideological justification of the seizure, the Muscovite authorities received a ready-made historical canon: "the 'SYNOPSIS,' 'Synopsis, or a Brief Collection from Various Chroniclers on the Origin of the Slaveno-Rossian People' – a historical work first published by the printing house of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra (1674)"[1] – the book constructed a shared "Slaveno-Rossian" past, legitimizing Moscow's rule over Kyiv retroactively.

T0007 Imposition of Its Own Picture of Reality

Imposition of Its Own Picture of Reality Tsardom of Muscovy Moscow Patriarchate

The implantation of a narrative about the unbreakable bond between Kyiv and Moscow, serving the political aims of the metropole: the author sought "to give the Muscovite tsar the motivation to continue the struggle with the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth" and "to ease the incorporation of the Hetmanate's elite into the Russian ruling estate"[2].

Procedure page →C0006.T0007

T0010 Denial of a Distinct Identity

Denial of a Distinct Identity Tsardom of Muscovy Moscow Patriarchate

The deliberate dissolution of a distinct Ukrainian identity in the construct of a "single Orthodox people": the "Synopsis" subordinated Kyiv's history to the idea of a common "Slaveno-Rossian people"[1], and its political program presupposed a struggle for the "liberation… of the remaining part of the 'single Orthodox people'"[2]. Hrushevsky points out what exactly was erased: the "Synopsis" is "a brief history of Rus composed at the Pechersk Lavra, done very lifelessly and incompletely (the history of the Cossacks, the struggle against Poland, and all of Ukraine's recent history are almost entirely omitted from it)"[3].

Procedure page →C0006.T0010

T0024 Justification Through Religion

Justification Through Religion Tsardom of Muscovy Moscow Patriarchate

The imperial historical myth was directly based on religious manipulation: expansion was justified by the concept of religious unity. The author constructed an ideologeme in order "to give the Muscovite tsar the motivation to continue the struggle with the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth for the liberation from Catholic rule of the remaining part of the 'single Orthodox people'"[2].
