Non-Recognition and the Attempted Military Invasion: Moscow's Response to the Independence of the Chechen Republic (September-November 1991)

After their return from exile the Chechens were ruled for three decades by Russians sent from Moscow; only on 1 July 1989 did an assembly of the region's occupation administration for the first time reject Moscow's candidate and choose a Chechen, Zavgaev, as head of the region[1]. By the autumn of 1991 the USSR was falling apart: the union republics were declaring independence one after another. On 18 August the USSR vice-president Yanaev, together with the heads of the KGB (predecessor of today's FSB), the army and the police, tried to keep the union in Moscow's hands by force: they announced that the nominal head of the USSR, Gorbachev, could not govern "for reasons of health", declared a state of emergency and brought tanks into Moscow, but gave up after three days[2]. Zavgaev, the head of Chechnya installed under the USSR, backed those who wanted to preserve the USSR, and lost: the Chechens came out to non-stop rallies and on 6 September forced his Supreme Soviet to dissolve itself[3]. That same autumn the Chechens elected their own president, Dzhokhar Dudaev, and declared independence from Russia, while the coloniser in the person of the RSFSR went in two months from sending a delegation "to explain the policy" to an attempt to impose a state of emergency on the whole territory of Chechen-Ingushetia with internal troops, but was forced to retreat. On 10 September 1991 the RSFSR president Yeltsin sent to Grozny a delegation headed by state secretary Burbulis and instructed it "to explain the policy of the Russian Federation and of the President of the RSFSR"[4]. On 14 September Khasbulatov, a Chechen from the Moscow leadership, then acting head of parliament, flew to Grozny; on 15 September he personally chaired the last session of the Supreme Soviet of Chechen-Ingushetia, and under pressure from Khasbulatov and from Dzhokhar Dudaev's supporters the deputies dismissed Zavgaev, dissolved themselves and chose from their own ranks a Provisional Supreme Council of 32 members headed by Khusein Akhmadov to hold new elections[5]. Moscow tried to take this council for itself: on 25 September five of its members headed by the council's deputy chairman Chernyi, Khasbulatov's man, came out against Dzhokhar Dudaev's congress; on 26 September Khasbulatov himself threatened by telegram not to recognise the elections, and on 5 October thirteen of the council's thirty-two members, backed by the local KGB, tried to remove Khusein Akhmadov. In response Dzhokhar Dudaev's supporters blocked the central square of Grozny, arrested the republic's procurator and occupied the buildings of the KGB and of the government; Moscow's people in the council, as general Sokolov reported to his command, had no "practical power" left[5][3]. On 6 October the RSFSR vice-president Rutskoi flew to Grozny with the minister of internal affairs and the chairman of the KGB of the RSFSR, but achieved nothing[5], and on 8 October the Moscow leadership, over Khasbulatov's signature, recognised as lawful in Chechnya only the group of procurator Bakhmadov - the man who, according to Sokolov's report, had been "appointed by the Chairman of the Supreme Soviet of the RSFSR Khasbulatov"[3] - and demanded that "the illegal armed formations [this is what the coloniser called the fighters for independence from Russia] surrender their weapons by 24:00 on 10 October 1991"[6]. On 24 October Moscow announced, before any vote had taken place, that the elections set for 27 October "have no legal basis and their results cannot be recognised as lawful"[7]. On 27 October the Chechens elected Dzhokhar Dudaev president, and on 1 November he signed a decree: "to proclaim the state sovereignty of the Chechen Republic from 1 November 1991"[8]. On 2 November Moscow declared the elections "unlawful, and the acts adopted by them not subject to execution"[9]. On 7 November Yeltsin, by decree No. 178, imposed a state of emergency on the whole territory of Chechen-Ingushetia: a curfew, a ban on rallies and strikes, the seizure of weapons, the cancellation of the elections, a Provisional Administration appointed from Moscow, and "the expulsion of violators of public order who are not residents of the given locality... beyond the territory of the Chechen-Ingush Republic at their own expense"[10]. On 8 November the parliament of the Chechen Republic declared the decree unlawful and demanded the withdrawal of the troops within 24 hours[11]. The National Guard of Chechnya and unarmed townspeople blockaded the airlifted internal troops (predecessors of today's Rosgvardiya) at the Khankala airfield near Grozny and in the building of the ministry of internal affairs. The nominal head of the USSR, Gorbachev, refused the RSFSR the use of union troops; on the morning of 10 November the internal troops left Grozny[12], and on 11 November the deputies in Moscow refused to approve Yeltsin's decree, finding it necessary to act "not by applying emergency measures but by political means"[13].

On 7 November 1991 the RSFSR president Yeltsin signed decree No. 178, point 1: "To introduce, from 05:00 on 9 November 1991 until 05:00 on 9 December 1991, a state of emergency on the whole territory of the Chechen-Ingush Republic". By the same decree he imposed a curfew, banned rallies and strikes, ordered "the temporary seizure of firearms and cold weapons from citizens, enterprises and organisations", searches of vehicles and dwellings, and "the expulsion of violators of public order who are not residents of the given locality to their place of permanent residence or beyond the territory of the Chechen-Ingush Republic at their own expense", and instructed the minister of internal affairs and the chairman of the KGB (predecessor of today's FSB) of the RSFSR to submit "proposals on bringing in additional forces"[10]. On 8 November the parliament of the Chechen Republic, chaired by Khusein Akhmadov, demanded that Yeltsin "within 24 hours withdraw all armed formations from the territory of the sovereign Chechen Republic" and resolved "to inform the parliaments and peoples of the world of the armed interference of the leadership of Russia in the internal affairs of the sovereign Chechen Republic"[11]. The journalist Tsyganok, in the article "Ten Days That Cancelled the Peace", quotes the summary of the RSFSR ministry of internal affairs for the morning of 10 November: "To ensure the regime of the state of emergency, units of the MVD [ministry of internal affairs] of the RSFSR with a total strength of about 1.2 thousand men have been sent to the North Caucasus region"[12]. According to the chronicle of events, on 8-9 November "two military transport aircraft with special-forces detachments on board, but without weapons, landed at the Khankala military airfield near Grozny… the airfield is blockaded by Dzh. Dudaev's guardsmen, leaving the aircraft is impossible, so on the morning of 9 November the aircraft fly away"[3]. The National Guard of Chechnya and unarmed townspeople surrounded the building of the ministry of internal affairs in Grozny; on 9 November the first deputy minister of internal affairs of the RSFSR Komissarov reported to Moscow: "We are blockaded in the building of the MVD [ministry of internal affairs] of the ChIR [Chechen-Ingush Republic]. We are surrounded by a crowd of about 4,000 people, fuel tankers are standing opposite"[12]. On 9 November the Moscow leadership recorded that "the heads of the executive bodies (Barannikov [minister of internal affairs of the USSR], Bakatin [chairman of the KGB of the USSR], Komissarov [first deputy minister of internal affairs of the RSFSR]) in a difficult critical situation failed to carry out their direct official duties"[14]. The nominal head of the USSR, Gorbachev, refused the RSFSR the use of union troops, and on 10 November at 10:45 the internal troops (predecessors of today's Rosgvardiya) began their withdrawal from Grozny[12]. On 11 November the deputies in Moscow resolved "to recognise the approval of the Decree as impossible", established "a special regime of entry into and exit from the Chechen-Ingush Republic, excluding the import and export of weapons" and ordered "a parliamentary and an internal (through the MVD of the RSFSR and the KGB of the RSFSR) investigation to establish the persons responsible for taking insufficiently prepared political and military-technical decisions on the introduction of the state of emergency"[13].

On 10 September 1991 the RSFSR president Yeltsin sent to Chechen-Ingushetia a delegation headed by state secretary Burbulis and instructed it "to explain the policy of the Russian Federation and of the President of the RSFSR at the present stage"[4]; the delegation came back with nothing[3]. On 8 October the Moscow leadership, over Khasbulatov's signature, resolved "until the election of a new composition of the Supreme Soviet of the Chechen-Ingush Republic, to regard the Provisional Supreme Council of the Republic as the only lawful body of state power on the territory of the Chechen-Ingush Republic" and ordered "the illegal armed formations [this is what the coloniser called the fighters for independence from Russia] to surrender their weapons to the bodies of internal affairs by 24:00 on 10 October 1991"[6]. The Chechens did not surrender their weapons: on 9 October the leadership of the National Congress of the Chechen People replied that the resolution had been adopted "with the sole aim of preserving colonial domination over the Chechen people, over the peoples of the Caucasus and their natural riches", announced the mobilisation of men from 15 to 55 and put the National Guard on combat alert[15]. On 10 October Moscow instructed vice-president Rutskoi and the RSFSR government "to ensure the necessary conditions for the restoration of legality and law and order in the Chechen-Ingush Republic"[16]. On 15 October the chairman of the KGB of the RSFSR (predecessor of today's FSB) Ivanenko suspended the work of the KGB of Chechen-Ingushetia "until 27 October 1991" and by his own order appointed an acting chairman for it[3]. On 19 October Yeltsin addressed the leadership of the congress with an ultimatum: "I demand from the leaders of the Executive Committee of the National Congress of the Chechen People and the groupings supporting them the cessation of unlawful actions and unconditional submission to the law… all measures provided for by the laws of the RSFSR will be taken"[17]. On 24 October Moscow resolved that the elections set for 27 October "have no legal basis and their results cannot be recognised as lawful", and entrusted the preparation of different elections to the Provisional Supreme Council and to the RSFSR deputies from the republic[7]. The Chechens voted anyway: on 27 October, according to the electoral commission of the Chechen Republic, 72 % of voters turned out, and 90.1 % voted for Dzhokhar Dudaev[3]. On 1 November Dzhokhar Dudaev signed a decree: "Guided by the Declaration on the state sovereignty of the republic and by the will of the citizens of the Chechen Republic expressed in direct and universal elections, to proclaim the state sovereignty of the Chechen Republic from 1 November 1991"[8]. On 2 November Moscow answered with a resolution: "To recognise the elections to the supreme body of state power (the Supreme Soviet) and of the President of the republic held in the Chechen-Ingush Republic on 27 October 1991 as unlawful, and the acts adopted by them as not subject to execution"[9]. On 7 November Yeltsin, in decree No. 178, named as the reason for the state of emergency an aggravation "caused by the unlawful actions of the executive committee of the National Congress of the Chechen People and its leaders, who seek, by organising mass disorders, with the use of violence, to remove the lawfully acting state bodies from power", and in point 6 banned any elections: "elections of bodies of state power and of local self-government shall not be held during the whole period of the state of emergency"[10]. None of these bans worked: the elections took place, the president and the parliament of Chechnya were at work, and on 11 November the deputies in Moscow refused to approve the decree itself[13].

S0008 Government

On 15 September 1991 the deputies of the Supreme Soviet of Chechen-Ingushetia, at their last session, chaired in person by Khasbulatov, a Chechen from the Moscow leadership, then acting head of parliament, dissolved themselves and chose from their own ranks a Provisional Supreme Council of 32 members headed by Khusein Akhmadov to hold new elections[5]. Filatov, in 1991 secretary of the parliament's leadership in Moscow, recalled: "with the energetic participation of Khasbulatov the Supreme Soviet of the republic was dispersed — what remained was the so-called Small Council, some artificial formation that was to work until new elections were held"[18]. Khusein Akhmadov took the side of Dzhokhar Dudaev's congress and on 4 October signed a resolution on the KGB (predecessor of today's FSB): "to terminate the activity of the KGB of the ChIR [Chechen-Ingush Republic] and of all its structures on the territory of the Chechen Republic"[19]. Then Moscow began to build its own power out of this council. On 25 September five of its members headed by the council's deputy chairman Chernyi, Khasbulatov's man, came out against Dzhokhar Dudaev's congress; on 26 September Khasbulatov himself threatened by telegram not to recognise the elections; on 5 October thirteen of the council's thirty-two members, backed by the local KGB, tried to remove Khusein Akhmadov[5]. On 8 October the Moscow leadership, over Khasbulatov's signature, having heard vice-president Rutskoi back from Grozny, declared lawful in the republic only the council "under the chairmanship of comrade B. D. Bakhmadov" - a procurator whom they themselves had installed - and instructed it "to take all necessary measures to stabilise the situation"[6]. General Sokolov, head of the occupation army's training centre in Grozny, reported to his command: "the chairman V. D. Bakhmadov was appointed by the Chairman of the Supreme Soviet of the RSFSR R. I. Khasbulatov", and the council itself "has no practical power"[3]. On 9 October the leadership of the National Congress of the Chechen People, Dzhokhar Dudaev's congress, called Bakhmadov's council "unlawful, puppet" and demanded that it "dissociate itself from the attempt to resurrect" the Supreme Soviet "dissolved by the people"[15]. After the elections of 27 October Bakhmadov's council addressed the residents of the republic: the elections "are unconstitutional and have no lawful force, not a single decree of the so-called president D. Dudaev, not a single resolution of the parliament of the so-called Chechen Republic has legal force or is subject to execution"[20]. On 7 November the RSFSR president Yeltsin, by decree No. 178, resolved: "To introduce... on the territory of the Chechen-Ingush Republic, as a special form of administration, a Provisional Administration of the Chechen-Ingush Republic"; as its head he appointed his own representative in the republic, Arsanov, as deputy the republic's minister of internal affairs Ibragimov, and instructed them "to form the Provisional Administration of the Chechen-Ingush Republic within 3 days"[10]. On 8 November the parliament of the Chechen Republic, chaired by Khusein Akhmadov, replied: "The leadership of Russia, following the lead of the overthrown totalitarian forces and proceeding from imperial interests, has introduced it [the state of emergency] on the territory of a republic whose sovereignty was proclaimed as far back as November 1990" - and declared the decree "unlawful and having no legal force"[11]. Neither Bakhmadov's council nor Arsanov's Provisional Administration ever got to work in Chechnya: on 11 November the deputies in Moscow refused to approve Yeltsin's decree[13].
