Staging a Coup

Aggressors may initiate, sponsor, and covertly coordinate a violent change of power in the colonized society. By overthrowing the legitimate government using the forces of internal opposition, radical groups, or recruited military officers, the colonizer artificially creates a political crisis. This allows it to swiftly install a puppet regime without having to launch an open, full-scale military intervention.

Procedures Carried Out


December 1917
C0072.T0020 →
C0072 Soviet-Ukrainian War: First Invasion and the Puppet Government (December 1917 - April 1918)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

In January 1918, while the troops of Soviet Russia were advancing on Kyiv, the Bolsheviks raised an uprising inside the city itself. On Sunday 15 (28) January, workers and armed Bolshevik detachments seized the "Arsenal" plant in Pechersk (a district of Kyiv)[1]. The next day, 16 (29) January, the Kyiv committee of the Bolshevik party issued a printed leaflet to the workers and soldiers of the city against the General Secretariat (the government of the Ukrainian People's Republic under the Central Rada). It said: "The gentlemen from the General Secretariat are preparing here, in the heart of Ukraine, the dispersal of the Soviets of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies and think that soldiers will be found here who will carry out this black deed. No, this shall not be!"[2]. The leaflet called "to the defence of the revolution" and ended with the appeal "Down with the counter-revolutionaries grouped in the Central Rada and the General Secretariat". On 19 January (1 February) Ordzhonikidze, the extraordinary commissar of Soviet Russia in Ukraine, telegraphed the government of Soviet Russia: "In Kyiv there is an uprising of workers and of soldiers of the Bohdan Khmelnytsky and Shevchenko regiments. The Arsenal is in the hands of the workers. Today Soviet troops will enter Kyiv"[3]. The uprising inside the city and the offensive from outside stand in one telegram and in one calculation.

August 1991
C0095.T0020 →
C0095 Attempted Coup d'État by the GKChP (August 1991)
G0010 USSR
S0008 Government

An armed seizure of power: the GKChP statement of 18 August 1991 announced the transfer of the USSR president's powers: "In connection with the impossibility, for reasons of health, of Mikhail Sergeevich Gorbachev performing the duties of President of the USSR and the transfer… of the powers of the President of the USSR to Vice-President Gennady Ivanovich Yanayev… to form the State Committee for the State of Emergency in the USSR"[4]. The putsch was timed to derail the treaty-based reconstitution of the Union: "The formation of the GKChP was preceded by the so-called Novo-Ogaryovo process - negotiations begun on April 23, 1991 by USSR President Gorbachev at his suburban residence of Novo-Ogaryovo with the leaders of the union republics on the signing of a new Union Treaty"[5] - the coup cut this process short.

September 1994
C1193.T0020 →
C1193 First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

In the autumn of 1994, Russia attacked the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria under the name of a puppet opposition, and on 11 December 1994 the Russian army openly invaded the republic. Russian special services and the army gave the puppet opposition money, weapons, instructors, tank crewmen and pilots in order to overthrow Dzhokhar Dudayev, the elected president of the Chechen Republic.

In the summer of 1994, Russia's Ministry for Nationalities Affairs and the FSK (a predecessor of today's FSB) helped the puppet opposition recruit armed fighters in Znamenskoye, a village in the northwest of Ichkeria, and in Urus-Martan, a town southwest of Grozny[6]. Russia allocated more than 150 billion rubles to the Provisional Council, the leadership of the puppet opposition, which it had confirmed at a congress on 3 June. In an analytical memorandum, the FSK named the head of the Provisional Council the most suitable candidate for support[6].

On 3 September 1994, the Russian government, in an address to the Chechen people, proposed that Dzhokhar Dudayev resign[6].

On 5 September 1994, the General Staff of the Russian army ordered that 10 armored personnel carriers and 6 combat helicopters with crews be handed over to the Provisional Council[6]. From these helicopters, Russian pilots fired rockets at residential houses in Ichkeria (see: "Indiscriminate Attacks"). In November, the Russian army handed tanks over to the council[6][7].

In September and October 1994, Russian officers trained more than a hundred fighters of the Provisional Council to drive T-62 tanks and fire army mortars at the "Prudboy" training ground near Volgograd, a city in the south of Russia[8][6].

In October 1994, Savostyanov, the head of the Moscow directorate of the FSK, ordered that Russian soldiers and officers who knew how to maintain armored vehicles and fight in them be found for the Provisional Council. The military counterintelligence of the FSK selected the candidates[9]. From 3 to 9 November, FSK officers travelled around military units near Moscow and signed contracts with tank crewmen[6][9].

In November 1994, Kolesnikov, the Chief of the General Staff of the Russian army, authorized sending the recruits to the Caucasus. Kotenkov, Russia's deputy minister for nationalities affairs, directed the dispatch, and by 16 November the recruits had been brought to Mozdok, a town in Russian-controlled North Ossetia near the border of the Chechen Republic[6]. Russia denied that it was sending its servicemen to fight for the Provisional Council (see: "Lies about Its Own Actions").

In the autumn of 1994, FSK officers fought on the side of the puppet opposition[6].

From September 1994 until the assault on Grozny on 26 November, fighters of the Provisional Council and Russian crews attacked Ichkeria. On 2 September, fighters of the council and a column of twelve tanks with Russian crews attacked Grozny. The Armed Forces of Ichkeria stopped them at the settlement of Dolinsky, northwest of Grozny, knocked out a tank and took eight men prisoner, among them, according to initial reports, Russian servicemen[10][11][12][6][13][14]. On 30 September, Russian pilots in four helicopters of the Provisional Council fired rockets at the "Severny" airport in Grozny and destroyed five civilian aircraft and two aircraft of the army of Ichkeria, and fighters of the puppet opposition blocked the entrances to the city[6][15][16]. Dzhokhar Dudayev rejected the Provisional Council's ultimatum to surrender within six hours, and the council's fighters dispersed without attacking Grozny[15][17]. On 15 October, fighters of the Provisional Council stormed Grozny from the south and from the north, and the Armed Forces of Ichkeria repelled the assault. The defenders took several dozen attackers prisoner, among them, according to various reports, 12 to 15 Russian officers[17], and on 16 October the council's fighters retreated to their bases in Znamenskoye and in Gekhi, a village southwest of Grozny[6].

On 17 November 1994, nine days before the assault on Grozny, a group of Russian officers headed by Kolesnikov, the Chief of the General Staff, flew from Moscow to Mozdok[6]. Valery Yakov, special correspondent of the Moscow newspaper Izvestia, wrote about the preparation of the assault in November 1996: "There, in Mozdok, at the 'Arsenal' base, the contract soldiers [recruited Russian servicemen]… trained the Chechen crews [of the Provisional Council]… In all, the contract soldiers received 40 brand-new tanks, 16 of which had 'Russian' crews"[18][9]. On 26 November, fighters of the Provisional Council and Russian tank crewmen under the command of Zhukov, the deputy commander of a Russian army corps from Volgograd, stormed Grozny[6][9]. Zelimkhan Yandarbiyev, the vice president of the Chechen Republic, wrote in his memoirs that in the morning he saw the start of the assault from the window of his office in the presidential palace: "on the northeastern outskirts of the city, from the direction of Tolstoy-Yurt [a village north of Grozny where the fighters of the Provisional Council were stationed], tank and artillery fire began to thunder"[19]. The tanks reached the center of Grozny, where fighters of the Armed Forces of Ichkeria shot them up with grenade launchers[6]. The defenders routed the attackers and, according to the government of the Chechen Republic, took 68 Russian servicemen prisoner[20]. Among the prisoners were soldiers and officers of the Russian Kantemirovskaya Tank Division from near Moscow[21].

Human rights defenders of Memorial write that without Russia's military help the puppet opposition could not have overthrown Dzhokhar Dudayev[6].

After the defeat of 26 November, the Provisional Council withdrew its fighters to its bases[22][6]. Fifteen days later, on 11 December 1994, the Russian army openly invaded the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria (see: "Military Intervention")[6].

April 2014
C0101.T0020 →
C0101 Continuation of the Russo-Ukrainian War: Armed Aggression in the Donbas (2014–2015)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

An attempted armed seizure of power in the spring of 2014 by storming regional administrations: the UN recorded that already "in mid-April 2014" "groups of armed men unlawfully carried out a well-organized and coordinated seizure of public buildings and police and security service offices in towns and villages of the Donetsk and Luhansk regions," and "in order to retain control of the seized areas these groups set up barricades and checkpoints"[23]. In Kharkiv the coup was quickly suppressed.
