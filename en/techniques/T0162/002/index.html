Killing of Those Who Surrender

T0162.002 Defense Impairment Ukrainians

Aggressors may kill those who lay down their arms and surrender. This is prohibited by Article 23(c) of the Hague Regulations - "to kill or wound an enemy who, having laid down his arms... has surrendered" - and by the Rome Statute (Article 8(2)(b)(vi)).

Procedures Carried Out1

1919 Soviet-Ukrainian War: Second Invasion and Resource Depletion Ukrainians

Soviet-Ukrainian War: Second Invasion and Resource Depletion (1919) White Movement (AFSR) Regular Army

Don Cossacks of the White army of General Denikin killed Ukrainian Galician riflemen taken prisoner - the command of the Galician Army lodged a formal protest with the Whites' command, and the Galician Army's own military court confirmed that the killings of prisoners were fact. On 25 October 1919 the Supreme Command - the staff of the Galician Army (UHA) - sent, as recorded in its war diary, a mission to the Whites' command - otaman Lysniak, sotnyks Levytsky and Kurytsia - "who were to lodge a formal protest against the killings of our prisoners by the Don Cossacks"[1]. In the delegation's written commission the task was recorded thus: "first, to protest against the killing of our prisoners by Denikin's troops, as took place in Haisyn, and second, to discuss the matter of an exchange of prisoners." Otaman Lysniak testified in court: "In Vinnytsia I received the commission to go to the Staff of the Volunteer Army [Denikin's, the 'Whites'], hand over the said protest and negotiate an exchange of prisoners"; the delegation was received by general Slashchov, who promised "to telephone Denikin"[1]. In November 1919, at the military trial of UHA commander general Tarnavsky, sotnyk Kurdiak asked "whether the killings by the Don Cossacks were fact or fiction"; Tarnavsky replied: "It is fact. Witness - otaman Bukshovany, and indeed the 5th Brigade"[1].
