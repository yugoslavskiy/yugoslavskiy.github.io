Rewriting of History

Aggressors may initiate the creation and mass dissemination of artificial historical concepts designed to substantiate their claim to foreign territories. Constructing a new historical canon, in which facts are distorted and the chronology of events is tailored to the interests of the metropole, allows the aggressor to retroactively legalize the occupation, convincing both its own population and the local one of the historical inevitability and legality of the absorption.

Procedures Carried Out


January 1550
C1104.T0004 →
C1104 Religious Frontier and the Fabrication of Historical Right (1550–1721)
G0008 Tsardom of Muscovy
S0008 Government

The government of the Tsardom of Muscovy fabricated historical narratives to provide legal justification for the invasion of new territories: historian Michael Khodarkovsky points out that in diplomatic correspondence officials deliberately constructed fictitious claims that the Indigenous peoples of the Caucasus had allegedly, since ancient times, been Muscovite "subjects in the Riazan region, who then fled from Riazan and settled in the mountains"[1].

January 1674
C0006.T0004 →
C0006 Publication of the "Synopsis" (1674)
G0008 Tsardom of Muscovy
S0011 Moscow Patriarchate

For the ideological justification of the seizure, the Muscovite authorities received a ready-made historical canon: "the 'SYNOPSIS,' 'Synopsis, or a Brief Collection from Various Chroniclers on the Origin of the Slaveno-Rossian People' – a historical work first published by the printing house of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra (1674)"[2] – the book constructed a shared "Slaveno-Rossian" past, legitimizing Moscow's rule over Kyiv retroactively.

January 1721
C1108.T0004 →
C1108 Imposition of an Image of Backwardness and the “Civilizing” Mission (1721–1800s)
G0009 Russian Empire
S0008 Government

The government of the Russian Empire fabricated historical accounts to provide legal justification for invasion: historian Michael Khodarkovsky points out that in 1748 and in the 1770s officials deliberately constructed fictitious claims that the Indigenous peoples of the Caucasus had allegedly been Christians in the past, in order to contest the claims of other empires and "legitimize their efforts at their 're-Christianization'"[3].

January 1888
C0065.T0004 →
C0065 The Bohdan Khmelnytsky Monument (1888)
G0009 Russian Empire
S0008 Government

On the pedestal of the Khmelnytsky monument (1888) the empire cast a programmatic statement about the past: on the front side stood the phrase "We will to be under the tsar of the east, the Orthodox one," attributed to the Pereiaslav Rada, and on the right - the imperial formula "To Bohdan Khmelnytsky – one and indivisible Russia"[4]. Brought together, they presented the events of 1654 as a voluntary and final choice in favor of a single Russian state, and the entire history of the Hetmanate as the road to that choice.

January 1933
C0079.T0004 →
C0079 Cultural Terror and the "Executed Renaissance" (1933–1938)
G0010 USSR
S0021 Propaganda

The construction of a new Soviet-imperial historical canon to supplant the Ukrainian past: "from the late 1930s a thesis took hold in the historical scholarship of the USSR," proclaimed in the works of B. Grekov, K. Huslystyi, and F. Yastrebov, "of Kyivan Rus as the 'common cradle' of the history of the Ukrainian, Russian, and Belarusian peoples," and "in 1939 B. Grekov advanced the proposition of the ethnic unity of the 'Rus people' in the era of Kyivan Rus"[5]. This scheme displaced M. Hrushevsky's conception and denied the independence of Ukraine's history.

January 1954
C0087.T0004 →
C0087 The Ideological Campaign of the "300th Anniversary of Reunification" and the Transfer of Crimea (1954)
G0010 USSR
S0021 Propaganda

A state campaign to construct a historical myth: the "Theses on the 300th Anniversary of the Reunification of Ukraine with Russia," approved by the CPSU Central Committee, declared that "by this historic act was completed the long struggle of the freedom-loving Ukrainian people against foreign enslavers for reunification with the Russian people in a single Russian state"[6] – the annexation of 1654 was retroactively rewritten as the goal and outcome of a national struggle.

January 1965
C1182.T0004 →
C1182 "Only Pardoned": Alien Rule and the Suppression of Chechen and Ingush National Life (1965-1989)
G0010 USSR
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

From 1973 the coloniser justified the deportation and did not let the Chechens speak of it or of the military seizure of the region, and from 1979 it imposed a "concept" of "the 200th anniversary of the voluntary entry of Chechen-Ingushetia into Russia" and persecuted the historians who disputed it. At a meeting of the region's occupation administration on 27 March 1973 one of its leaders, Bokov, justified the eviction of 1944: "All this, in our view, forced the government to take this extreme measure in order to put an end to the anti-Soviet actions of the political bandit groups [the authorities' name for armed opponents], which found shelter among a significant part of the Chechen-Ingush population", and the minutes of the same meeting named the bearers of "nationalist" ideas one by one: "the writer Khalid Oshaev, expelled from the party, the former deputy director of a plant Kostoev B., the university lecturer and candidate of sciences Vagapov Ya."[7]. Historian A. B. Kuznetsova writes that at the discussion of the two-volume "Essays on the History of the Chechen-Ingush ASSR" in 1973 the speakers criticised the "belittling" of the role of the Russian people and the works of the historian V. I. Filkin, "while not a word was said at the discussion about the deportation"[8]. Historians Ya. Z. Akhmadov and E. Kh. Khasmagomadov write that the region's occupation administration launched a persecution of the Chechen intelligentsia "under the guise of a discussion around the 'concept' of the 200th anniversary of the voluntary entry of Chechen-Ingushetia into Russia", whose author was considered to be the archaeologist Vinogradov, and he "relied even on the authority of the CC of the CPSU [the central authority in Moscow]"[9]. In February 1979 L. I. Lavrov, a historian of the Academy of Sciences, wrote on instructions from his superiors a review of the "Justification Memorandum" for the jubilee: the oaths of the 18th century "did not lead to the actual entry of the region into the Russian Empire, since even after the oaths no officials of the tsarist administration appeared there", and "the planned celebration of the jubilee, in the light of the above, appears historically unjustified"; historian V. P. Krikunov, who published the memorandum in 1990, writes: "The first copy of the memorandum got lost and, when the question of whether or not to celebrate the 200th 'jubilee' was being decided, was not taken into account (or rather, it was taken into account, and that, evidently, is why it got lost)"[10]. Historian Viktor Shnirelman writes: "In those years Vinogradov became the chief adviser of the obkom of the CPSU [the region's occupation administration] on ideology and social sciences, which in effect made him the first censor of scholarly and publishing activity in the republic"; "Such Chechen and Ingush historians as M. N. Muzaev, A. Z. Vatsuev, Ya. Z. Akhmadov, Kh. Akiev, Ya. S. Vagapov, Kh. S. Akhmadov and others were subjected to persecution"; the 1977 book by the linguist Kyuri Chokaev, "The Language and History of the Vainakhs", "was declared in the obkom… a model of 'rabid nationalism', and the entire print run was destroyed on the spot"[11]. Historians D. B. Abdurakhmanov and Ya. Z. Akhmadov also write that for disagreeing with the "concept" "there were officially pilloried [publicly disgraced] … the Chechen-Ingush State University lecturer F. P. Bokov, professors N. P. Gritsenko and V. P. Krikunov"[12]. Shnirelman writes that Vlasov, head of the region's occupation administration, reproduced the concept "in his speech at the III plenum of the Chechen-Ingush obkom of the CPSU on 14 July 1979" and "gave instructions to propagate it widely in the local media", and in 1982 the jubilee of the "voluntary entry" was combined with the 60th anniversary of the republic: "On this occasion the republic was awarded the Order of the Red Banner of Labour; its leaders received awards too. In particular, Vlasov was presented with the Order of Lenin"[11]. On 29 June 1984 the region's occupation administration recorded in its minutes on the work of the Institute of History, Sociology and Philology: "Vagapov Ya. S., Akhmadov Ya. Z., Vatsuev A. Z. permit themselves politically harmful judgements, dispute the scholarly validity of the concept of the voluntary entry of the Chechens and the Ingush into Russia"[12]. Historians D. B. Abdurakhmanov and Ya. Z. Akhmadov write that only in 1990 did "the same organ of the CPSU revoke its decision concerning the institute and its staff as mistaken"[12].

January 2020
C0102.T0004 →
C0102 Ideological Preparation for the Full-Scale Invasion (2020–2021)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0009 Diplomacy

The publication of the manifesto "On the Historical Unity of Russians and Ukrainians" (July 2021), constructing a pseudo-historical myth: "Russians and Ukrainians are one people, a single whole," while modern borders are declared the result of the fact that "the Bolsheviks' actions in severing from Russia its historical territories are not considered a criminal act"[13]. The same article describes how Lenin personally "persuaded" the leaders of the Donetsk-Kryvyi Rih republic "to act within Soviet Ukraine," and how the Central Committee resolved "to create at the congress 'one government for all of Ukraine'"[13]. On the eve of the invasion the thesis was taken to its limit: in his address of February 21, 2022 Putin declared that "modern Ukraine was entirely and wholly created by Russia, or, to be more precise, by Bolshevik, communist Russia," and called the country "Ukraine named after Vladimir Ilyich Lenin" - "He is its author and architect"[14].
