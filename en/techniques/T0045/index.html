Destruction of Historical Memory

Sub-techniques (2)
ID Name
T0045.001 Destruction of Shrines and Necropolises
T0045.002 Burning of Books and Archives

Aggressors may deliberately destroy the physical and intellectual objects that shape national identity: museums, architectural monuments, cultural institutions, archives and written heritage, shrines and necropolises. Erasing material evidence of the past and forcibly removing national symbols from public space allows the colonizer to create a cultural vacuum, which is subsequently filled, with no alternative, by the artificial narratives of the metropole.

October 1708

C0012 Mazepa's Defection to Sweden and the Baturyn Massacre (1708)
G0008 Tsardom of Muscovy
S0008 Government

A campaign to erase the resistance leader's name (damnatio memoriae): "For two centuries the name and deeds of Mazepa were expunged… His coats of arms were knocked off the churches he had built, his name was painted over in books"[1][2].

January 1709
C0014.T0045 →
C0014 Liquidation of the Zaporozhian Sich (1709)
G0008 Tsardom of Muscovy
S0008 Government

The issuing of an official manifesto banning the very name of the Sich in any documents[2]. Physical destruction of memory: "the graves of old Zaporozhians were desecrated"[1].

January 1718
C0042.T0045 →
C0042 Destruction of the Archives of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra (1718)
G0008 Tsardom of Muscovy
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

A covert operation by Muscovite emissaries disguised as monks to destroy ancient documents of the 11th–17th centuries: "The enormous archive and library burned down; the charters and grants… of princes, magnates, and hetmans perished"; "The fire destroyed the written memory of the Lavra's independent Ukrainian political and spiritual life"[3].

May 1775
C0033.T0045 →
C0033 Military Liquidation of the Zaporozhian Sich (1775)
G0009 Russian Empire
S0008 Government

The issuing of Catherine II's August manifesto, officially erasing the very memory of the Sich: it was destroyed "with the extirpation for the future of the very name of the Zaporozhian Cossacks," for the empire resolved "to destroy the Zaporozhian Sich and the name of the Cossacks derived from it"[4]. Hrushevsky: "The Sich was destroyed, and on 3 August a tsarist decree announced its abolition, 'with the annihilation of the very name of the Zaporozhian Cossacks'"[5].

January 1913
C0051.T0045 →
C0051 Ban on Culture and the Press Before World War I (1913–1914)
G0009 Russian Empire
S0008 Government

The erasure of the memory of national heroes: "In January 1914, minister of internal affairs Nikolai Maklakov signed a circular by which he forbade throughout all governorates of the empire 'public celebrations of the Little Russian writer Taras Shevchenko'"[6].

January 1933
C0079.T0045 →
C0079 Cultural Terror and the "Executed Renaissance" (1933–1938)
G0010 USSR
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

Institutional destruction of national historical scholarship, closure of research schools, and removal of the works of Ukrainian historians from scholarly circulation[7].

February 1943
C1180.T0045 →
C1180 Operation "Chechevitsa": the Total Deportation of the Chechen People and the Special Settlement Regime (1943-1949)
G0010 USSR
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

On the land emptied by the deportation of the Chechens the colonizer destroyed the urban markers of their history: it ransacked the republican local history museum and blew up the monument to the Chechen commander Aslanbek Sheripov[8].

September 1994
C1193.T0045 →
C1193 First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0010 Regular Army

In the autumn of 1994, Russia attacked the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria under the name of a puppet opposition, and on 11 December 1994 the Russian army openly invaded the republic. The occupiers destroyed the museums, research institutes and ancient monuments of the Chechens.

During the storming of Grozny, Russian artillerymen shelled, and pilots bombed, the center of the city, where the museums and research institutes of Ichkeria stood[9][10]. Russian pilots fired rockets at ancient towers and settlements in the mountains in the south of Ichkeria[10]. The occupiers occupied the ruins of museums and looted what had survived (see: "Theft of Cultural Property")[10]. Russian troops burned almost the entire Central State Archive of the Chechen Republic and the republican libraries in Grozny (see: "Burning of Books and Archives")[9]. Soldiers of the occupation troops destroyed mosques and leveled ancient burial mounds (see: "Destruction of Shrines and Necropolises")[10].

In January 1995, Russian troops destroyed the Chechen National Museum in Grozny. The museum held about 320 thousand exhibits, among them archaeological finds of the ancient and medieval Caucasus, traditional Chechen clothing and old manuscripts[9][10]. The museum's director Isa Adayev told the public tribunal of the Russian Glasnost Foundation in Prague in May 1996: "From 1 to 4 January 1995, the museum building was shelled quite deliberately from several directions". When the director got to the museum on 7 January, "it was no longer a museum — nothing was left of it — only ruins". On 18 January, Russian special forces seized the ruins[10]. Khotin, deputy director of the museum center of the Russian Ministry of Culture, who inspected the ruins of the museum in late March 1995, told the public tribunal of the Glasnost Foundation in Moscow in February 1996: "The nature of the destruction shows that barrage fire was used there. Next to the museum there is a small park of about 50 fairly old trees. Well, all those trees are cut in half. All of them!"[11].

In early 1995, the occupiers destroyed in Grozny the Research Institute of the Humanities of the Chechen Republic, founded in 1907[9]. In 1995, Russian pilots fired rockets at and bombed Kogo-Maista, an old settlement in the mountains in the south of Ichkeria, far from the fighting. In the strikes, the occupiers destroyed six towers that had stood for thirteen centuries, killed the Chechen boy Deni Murtazov and wounded a woman of his family so badly that she became disabled[10].

The human rights defenders of "Memorial" write that Russian troops destroyed or irreparably damaged in Grozny the university, the pedagogical and petroleum institutes, and the geological museum. In "Memorial's" assessment, Russian troops did not take the rescue of cultural property into account and inflicted irreparable damage on the culture of the Chechen people[9].

In August 1996, the Armed Forces of Ichkeria liberated Grozny from the occupiers, forcing Russia to the negotiating table[12]. On 31 August 1996, peace agreements were signed in Khasavyurt (see: "Sham Treaties")[13]. On 31 December 1996, the last Russian troops left the republic[9].
