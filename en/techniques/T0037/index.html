Puppet Government

Aggressors may artificially create and install fully controlled organs of power in occupied territories. Forming a puppet government allows the aggressor to simulate an internal civil conflict for the initial seizure of governance, and then to rule the region by proxy for long-term institutional consolidation.

Procedures Carried Out


October 1708
C0012.T0037 →
C0012 Mazepa's Defection to Sweden and the Baturyn Massacre (1708)
G0008 Tsardom of Muscovy
S0010 Regular Army

The installation of a government fully controlled by Moscow after the destruction of the capital: the election of the new hetman took place in Peter's presence immediately after the execution of the Baturyn officers – "Under the circumstances, the officers simply confirmed Skoropadsky, presented by Peter"[1]. Hrushevsky records the mechanics of dependence: "The hetman's residence was moved from Baturyn to Hlukhiv, right up against the Muscovite border, and two Muscovite regiments were garrisoned there so that the residents could arrest the hetman and the officers at once," and "everyone in Ukraine understood that power lay not with the hetman but with those residents"[2].

December 1917
C0072.T0037 →
C0072 Soviet-Ukrainian War: First Invasion and the Puppet Government (December 1917 - April 1918)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

In December 1917 the Bolsheviks gathered their own congress of soviets in Kharkiv and created a government subordinate to Petrograd, the "People's Secretariat". The goal was to pass off Russia's war against Ukraine as an internal struggle among Ukrainians. The Encyclopedia of the History of Ukraine describes it this way: it was created "as an alternative to the Ukrainian Central Rada and the General Secretariat of the Ukrainian Central Rada" and "acted in close contact with and under the direct leadership of the highest state and party institutions of Soviet Russia"[3]. The congress declared Ukraine a part of Russia. On 12 (25) December 1917 it adopted a resolution on the self-determination of Ukraine. In it the congress recognized "the Ukrainian Republic as a federative part of the Russian Republic" and declared "a resolute struggle against the policy of the Central Rada, ruinous for the worker and peasant masses, exposing its bourgeois, counter-revolutionary character"[4]. The congress explained its right to power by the coup in Petrograd and wrote in the resolution: "the principle of the right of nations to self-determination, proclaimed by the February Revolution, could be put into practice only thanks to the October Revolution, which led to the creation of the Worker-Peasant Government — the Council of People's Commissars"[4]. The puppet government in Kharkiv had no laws of its own: it introduced Petrograd's ready-made decrees in Ukraine. The congress itself decided so: "to immediately extend to the territory of the Ukrainian Republic all decrees and orders of the worker-peasant government of the Federation" and "to declare invalid all orders of the Rada that it has issued and will issue"[5]. The entire new "all-Ukrainian" Soviet authority consisted of 40 people chosen by the congress. There were no peasants among them: the congress left 20 seats for a peasant congress that had not yet convened[6]. Among its first decisions the puppet government appealed to the Council of People's Commissars (the government of Soviet Russia) and annulled the reply of the General Secretariat (the government of the Ukrainian People's Republic under the Central Rada) to Petrograd's ultimatum[6]. It explained the purpose of the appeal this way: "to avoid the possibility of a fratricidal war between the working peoples inhabiting Russia"[6]. The Council of People's Commissars had telegraphed this ultimatum to Kyiv earlier, on 4 (17) December[7]. It put four demands to the Rada. Not to break up the common front by recalling Ukrainian units. Not to let Cossack units pass to the Don, to Kaledin (a general, ataman of the Don Cossack Host, who did not recognize the authority of the Council of People's Commissars), without Petrograd's consent. To help the troops of Soviet Russia against Kaledin. To stop disarming Soviet regiments and the Red Guard. The Council of People's Commissars wrote in the ultimatum: "in the event that a satisfactory answer to these questions is not received within 48 hours, the Council of People's Commissars will consider the Rada to be in a state of open war against Soviet power in Russia and in Ukraine"[8]. The General Secretariat replied with a refusal the next day[7]. The puppet government withdrew that refusal. Thus Petrograd obtained a Ukrainian government that accepted the terms of the ultimatum. Petrograd also kept its own man inside the government. The people's secretary for inter-ethnic affairs, Bakinsky, was at the same time the representative of the Council of People's Commissars of Russia[3]. The historian Stanislav Kulchytsky describes direct supervision: "since no Ukrainian party centre existed, Lenin placed a temporary extraordinary commissar over the Kharkiv government"[9]. That commissar was Ordzhonikidze, an official of the central party apparatus[9]. The journal "Letopis revolyutsii" (a publication of Istpart, the party commission on history) wrote in 1924: "The all-Ukrainian government was a centre without a periphery, a headquarters without an army, when it had neither territory, nor a population subordinate to it, nor an armed force"[10].

January 1919
C0074.T0037 →
C0074 Soviet-Ukrainian War: Second Invasion and Resource Depletion (1919)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

On 28 November 1918 the Central Committee of the Bolshevik party (the CC RCP(b)) created in the Russian city of Kursk a puppet government for Ukraine, which named itself the "Provisional Workers' and Peasants' Government of Ukraine" - political cover for the invasion by the troops of Soviet Russia into Ukraine. By a manifesto of 29 November it proclaimed the establishment of Soviet power in Ukraine, and on 29 January 1919 it was reorganized into the Council of People's Commissars of the UkrSSR on the Russian model[11]. By a decree of 3 December 1918 - a month before the capture of Kharkiv - this government set up its own secret police: the All-Ukrainian Extraordinary Commission[12].
In the summer of 1919 Moscow took even the punitive apparatus away from the front it had created. On 11 June 1919 Shishkov, deputy chairman of the Ukrainian secret police, telegraphed the CC RCP(b) that it had no organizational ties whatsoever with the Russian one and that all local commissions in Ukraine were subordinate only to the Ukrainian, not the Moscow one[13]. On 22 July 1919 Dzerzhinsky, head of the Russian secret police, wrote to Latsis, head of the Ukrainian one, that the Ukrainian commission's business was to carry out in Ukraine the policy of the party's Moscow centre: "in relation to us You have become a samostiynik, become one objectively," and its task was "to be the conductor of the policy of the CC RCP [the Central Committee of the Russian Communist Party] in Ukraine"[14]. On 13 August 1919 the politburo of the CC RCP(b) resolved to dissolve the Ukrainian secret police, merging it with the Russian one, and charged Peters with the execution; on 14 August the politburo of the party's Ukrainian branch (the CC CP(b)U) submitted, and on 16 August Peters was appointed representative of the Russian commission attached to the colonial administration of Ukraine (the UkrSSR)[15].

February 1919
C1174.T0037 →
C1174 Denikin's terror in Chechnya: destroyed auls and 15 thousand dead (1919)
G0014 White Movement (AFSR)
S0010 Regular Army

In 1919 General Denikin refused to recognize the Mountain government and dissolved it[16]; General Lyakhov, appointed ruler of the Terek oblast, agreed to regard the republic's parliament as no more than "a consultative organ attached to his person"[17]. As ruler of Chechnya the Volunteer Army appointed a general from among the Chechens - historians Ya. Z. Akhmadov and E. Kh. Khasmagomadov write: "As Ruler of Chechnya on behalf of the Volunteer Army was appointed a well-known military man - General Eriskhan (Iriskhan) Aliev, a Chechen by origin"[17]. On 29 March 1919 Denikin convened a conference in Grozny - as the same historians write, one "called upon to put an end to the bloodshed and to confirm E. Aliev as ruler of Chechnya" - attended by representatives of 42 villages, mostly supporters of the Atagi Soviet (the Chechen National Council, which sat in Starye Atagi), entrepreneurs and part of the clergy[17].

September 1991
C1183.T0037 →
C1183 Non-Recognition and the Attempted Military Invasion: Moscow's Response to the Independence of the Chechen Republic (September-November 1991)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0008 Government

On 15 September 1991 the deputies of the Supreme Soviet of Chechen-Ingushetia, at their last session, chaired in person by Khasbulatov, a Chechen from the Moscow leadership, then acting head of parliament, dissolved themselves and chose from their own ranks a Provisional Supreme Council of 32 members headed by Khusein Akhmadov to hold new elections[18]. Filatov, in 1991 secretary of the parliament's leadership in Moscow, recalled: "with the energetic participation of Khasbulatov the Supreme Soviet of the republic was dispersed — what remained was the so-called Small Council, some artificial formation that was to work until new elections were held"[19]. Khusein Akhmadov took the side of Dzhokhar Dudaev's congress and on 4 October signed a resolution on the KGB (predecessor of today's FSB): "to terminate the activity of the KGB of the ChIR [Chechen-Ingush Republic] and of all its structures on the territory of the Chechen Republic"[20]. Then Moscow began to build its own power out of this council. On 25 September five of its members headed by the council's deputy chairman Chernyi, Khasbulatov's man, came out against Dzhokhar Dudaev's congress; on 26 September Khasbulatov himself threatened by telegram not to recognise the elections; on 5 October thirteen of the council's thirty-two members, backed by the local KGB, tried to remove Khusein Akhmadov[18]. On 8 October the Moscow leadership, over Khasbulatov's signature, having heard vice-president Rutskoi back from Grozny, declared lawful in the republic only the council "under the chairmanship of comrade B. D. Bakhmadov" - a procurator whom they themselves had installed - and instructed it "to take all necessary measures to stabilise the situation"[21]. General Sokolov, head of the occupation army's training centre in Grozny, reported to his command: "the chairman V. D. Bakhmadov was appointed by the Chairman of the Supreme Soviet of the RSFSR R. I. Khasbulatov", and the council itself "has no practical power"[22]. On 9 October the leadership of the National Congress of the Chechen People, Dzhokhar Dudaev's congress, called Bakhmadov's council "unlawful, puppet" and demanded that it "dissociate itself from the attempt to resurrect" the Supreme Soviet "dissolved by the people"[23]. After the elections of 27 October Bakhmadov's council addressed the residents of the republic: the elections "are unconstitutional and have no lawful force, not a single decree of the so-called president D. Dudaev, not a single resolution of the parliament of the so-called Chechen Republic has legal force or is subject to execution"[24]. On 7 November the RSFSR president Yeltsin, by decree No. 178, resolved: "To introduce... on the territory of the Chechen-Ingush Republic, as a special form of administration, a Provisional Administration of the Chechen-Ingush Republic"; as its head he appointed his own representative in the republic, Arsanov, as deputy the republic's minister of internal affairs Ibragimov, and instructed them "to form the Provisional Administration of the Chechen-Ingush Republic within 3 days"[25]. On 8 November the parliament of the Chechen Republic, chaired by Khusein Akhmadov, replied: "The leadership of Russia, following the lead of the overthrown totalitarian forces and proceeding from imperial interests, has introduced it [the state of emergency] on the territory of a republic whose sovereignty was proclaimed as far back as November 1990" - and declared the decree "unlawful and having no legal force"[26]. Neither Bakhmadov's council nor Arsanov's Provisional Administration ever got to work in Chechnya: on 11 November the deputies in Moscow refused to approve Yeltsin's decree[27].

September 1994
C1193.T0037 →
C1193 First Russian-Chechen War (1994–1996)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

In the autumn of 1994, Russia attacked the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria under the name of a puppet opposition, and on 11 December 1994 the Russian army openly invaded the republic. Russia installed governments under its control in Ichkeria.
On 23 November 1994 the Provisional Council, a puppet opposition, announced the creation of a "Government of National Revival" headed by Khadzhiyev[28]. The Russian army handed the Provisional Council armored vehicles and helicopters with crews for the storming of Grozny, the capital of Ichkeria, but on 26 November the Armed Forces of Ichkeria routed the attackers' troops (see: "Staging a Coup")[28].
On 17 December 1994, six days after the open invasion, Russian President Yeltsin, by Decree No. 2200, created an occupation administration in Ichkeria headed by Yegorov, the Russian minister for nationalities (see: "Installation of a New System of Governance")[28][29]. On 26 December the puppet opposition announced Khadzhiyev's "government" for the second time. The "government" declared that it was ready for talks with Russia and did not demand the withdrawal of Russian troops[28].
On 24 October 1995, two weeks after Moscow halted the talks with the leadership of Ichkeria, Russia replaced Khadzhiyev as head of the puppet government with Zavgayev, the last leader of the Soviet regime in Chechen-Ingushetia. On 14-17 December Russia held a "vote" under occupation and declared its protégé the "elected" head of the republic (see: "Sham Expression of Popular Will")[28].
At the end of January 1996 Chernomyrdin, the chairman of the Russian government, wrote to the deputies of the Russian parliament that the results of the "vote" showed support for Zavgayev in the republic, and proposed to resolve the war as a dispute among Chechens themselves[28].
On 27 May 1996 Russian President Yeltsin received in the Kremlin a delegation of Ichkeria headed by Zelimkhan Yandarbiyev, the acting president. Russia included Zavgayev in its delegation. The Chechen negotiators demanded that Moscow's protégé leave the table, but the Russian side refused. Russia insisted that the head of the puppet government sign the ceasefire agreement, and the delegation of Ichkeria objected again. All members of the Russian delegation, including Zavgayev, signed the document, as did the representatives of Ichkeria. A report by the NTV correspondents Cherkasov, Rebrov and Melikhov for the program "Itogi" says that the Russian protégé did not want to sign any agreements with Zelimkhan Yandarbiyev: "However, the center [Moscow] gave the order and the region [the puppet government] answered 'yes, sir'"[30].
On 14 July 1996 Russian President Yeltsin, by the decree "On Measures to Stabilize the Activities of the Bodies of State Power of the Chechen Republic", declared that the puppet Committee of National Accord and the "Government of National Revival" were no longer needed[31][28]. In their place Moscow recognized the "elected" Zavgayev, his government, the puppet parliament and the heads of districts as the leadership of the republic[28][31].
The human rights defenders of Memorial write that the Russian occupation administration in fact controlled the puppet government[28]. They write about Decree No. 435, by which Russian President Yeltsin on 31 March 1996 promised to halt the offensive, begin talks with Ichkeria and declare an amnesty: "all these measures were regarded as auxiliary in the process of legitimizing [recognizing as lawful] the puppet regime"[28]. Tim Guldimann, the head of the mission of the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe in Grozny from January 1996, wrote in 1998 that Moscow pushed the Armed Forces of Ichkeria southward with its troops and used Zavgayev's government to strengthen Russia's influence in the Chechen Republic[32].
On 31 July 1996, at a meeting with Chechens living in Moscow, Zavgayev, the head of the puppet government, said that the Armed Forces of Ichkeria did not control most of the districts of the republic[33]. On 6 August the army of Ichkeria attacked the occupiers in Grozny[28]. On 7 August Zavgayev fled to the base of the occupation troops in Khankala, on the eastern outskirts of the city[34], and then left the Chechen Republic[35]. At the end of August the police officers of the puppet government moved from their buildings to the Russian military at the Severny airport to the north of the capital[34]. The defenders liberated Grozny from the occupiers and forced Russia to the negotiating table. On 31 August 1996 Moscow signed peace agreements with the leadership of Ichkeria in Khasavyurt, a town in Russian-controlled Dagestan (see: "Sham Treaties")[36]. Until November 1996 the colonizer called the departed Zavgayev the "head of the republic"[28][37]. On 31 December 1996, the last Russian troops left Ichkeria. On 27 January 1997 the Chechens elected Aslan Maskhadov president of Ichkeria[28].

February 2014
C0100.T0037 →
C0100 Beginning of the Russo-Ukrainian War: Armed Invasion and Occupation of Crimea (2014)
G0011 Russian Federation
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

The installation of a controlled administration under military cover: the decisions of the Crimean parliament were taken in conditions where "behind the backs of Crimea's self-defense forces, our servicemen, of course, stood"[38]. The UN General Assembly declared the ensuing referendum void and confirmed that it "was not authorized by Ukraine"[39]. OHCHR recorded the "election" scene itself: overnight into 27 February "armed groups without insignia took over the buildings of the local government and parliament," and "on 27 February, members of the Parliament of Crimea, in the presence of gunmen, dismissed the local Government and elected Sergey Aksenov as the Head of Crimea"[40].
