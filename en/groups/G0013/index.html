Soviet Russia (RSFSR)

A state formed as a result of the Bolshevik coup in November 1917 (official name from July 1918: the Russian Socialist Federative Soviet Republic). It acted as the principal actor of military expansion, hybrid war, and the colonial absorption of the Ukrainian People's Republic (UNR) in 1917–1922. In December 1922 it became a co-founder and the core part of the USSR.

Procedures Carried Out


November 1917
T0131 Support for a Controlled Opposition
C0071 Failure of the Political Seizure of the UNR in Kyiv (November – December 1917)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0009 Diplomacy

Use by the Council of People's Commissars of local Bolshevik cells as a legal political force that was to speak on behalf of the Ukrainian proletariat against the Central Rada[1].

November 1917
T0132 Infiltration of Local Political Structures
C0071 Failure of the Political Seizure of the UNR in Kyiv (November – December 1917)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0020 Controlled Opposition

Deliberate transfer of loyal delegates and political agitators to the All-Ukrainian Congress of Soviets in Kyiv in order to hijack the agenda from within[2].

November 1917
T0133 Seizure of Power Through Election Manipulation
C0071 Failure of the Political Seizure of the UNR in Kyiv (November – December 1917)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0020 Controlled Opposition

An attempt to convert an electoral minority into a procedural majority at the congress. Actual support was negligible: in the Constituent Assembly elections "across the country as a whole the Bolsheviks gathered less than 24% of the votes, and in the Ukrainian gubernias only 10%"[2]. Hence the wager on capturing representative bodies: the Central Committee of the RSDLP(b) declared outright that "the transfer of government from the hands of one soviet party into the hands of another party is secured without any revolution, by a simple decision of the Soviets, by a simple re-election of deputies to the Soviets," while the All-Russian Central Executive Committee's decree of 4 December 1917 "On the right of recall of delegates" provided the mechanism for such a replacement[2][3].

December 1917
T0037 Puppet Government
C0072 Creation of a Puppet Government and Disguising the Intervention (December 1917 – Early 1918)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

The holding of an alternative congress in Kharkiv and the creation of a Moscow-controlled "People's Secretariat" to imitate an internal struggle: it was created "as an alternative to the Ukrainian Central Rada and the General Secretariat of the Ukrainian Central Rada" and "acted in close contact with and under the direct leadership of the highest state and party institutions of Soviet Russia"[4].

December 1917
T0018 Proxy War
C0072 Creation of a Puppet Government and Disguising the Intervention (December 1917 – Early 1918)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

The use of the Kharkiv government as formal cover for external aggression, to present the military invasion as a civil war inside Ukraine: the People's Secretariat "announced the overthrow of the UCR and the recognition of only its own decrees as valid. It introduced the decrees of the Council of People's Commissars of Russia in Ukraine"[4][1].

December 1917
T0020 Staging a Coup
C0072 Creation of a Puppet Government and Disguising the Intervention (December 1917 – Early 1918)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

The Bolsheviks' provocation of an uprising of the Arsenal plant workers in Kyiv itself at the very moment of the Red troops' offensive: it was "an armed action organized by the Kyiv committee of the Russian Social Democratic Workers' Party (Bolsheviks) against the Ukrainian Central Rada," and "it was planned for the moment of the approach of Soviet troops to Kyiv in the course of Soviet Russia's war against the UNR of 1917–18"[5].

December 1917
T0019 Military Intervention
C0072 Creation of a Puppet Government and Disguising the Intervention (December 1917 – Early 1918)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0010 Regular Army

The direct deployment of Red troops, heavy fighting for the stations, and the storming of Kyiv. The Battle of Kruty (January 1918) took place "during the offensive on the city of Kyiv by the troops of Bolshevik Russia": advancing against the cadets and the student company was "a 6-thousand-strong Bolshevik detachment of Petrograd and Moscow Red Guards," and the defenders' losses amounted to "up to 300 killed, wounded, captured"[6]. The march on Kyiv was led by M. Muravyov: he "headed the march of Soviet troops on Kyiv in January 1918"[4]. Overall command was exercised by V. Antonov-Ovseenko: "in December 1917 - March 1918 - commissar for combating counter-revolution in the South, he led the Soviet troops in the struggle against otaman A. Kaledin and the Ukrainian Central Rada"[7].

December 1917
T0104 Mass Killings of Civilians
C0072 Creation of a Puppet Government and Disguising the Intervention (December 1917 – Early 1918)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0010 Regular Army

The Muravyov massacre of February 1918 after the capture of Kyiv by the Red troops: the EIU characterizes Muravyov as "the initiator of mass executions in the city," and later he was himself arrested on the charge of "'condoning mass executions and lootings that led to the fall of Soviet power in Ukraine'"[4]. Hrushevsky on the campaign's commander: "the chief commandant, the former gendarme Muravyov, who in February had boasted of executions of Ukrainians and the ruin of their property"[8].

January 1918

C0073 Artificial Separatism and the Dismemberment of Territories (January – March 1918)

An attempt at the bureaucratic resubordination of territories — declaring the Donbas an autonomous part directly linked to Russia, bypassing any Ukrainian authorities[1].

January 1919
T0019 Military Intervention
C0074 Second Armed Invasion and Resource Depletion (1919)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0010 Regular Army

A full-scale offensive by the Ukrainian Front of the Red Army in early 1919 to recapture Ukrainian cities after the withdrawal of Austro-German troops[2]. The Red Army's counteroffensive in the fall and winter of 1919 against Denikin's forces, as a result of which the Bolsheviks recaptured Kyiv on December 16 and occupied most of Ukraine[2].
The invasion of General Denikin's troops under the slogan of restoring a "united and indivisible Russia" and the armed ousting of Ukrainian units from Kyiv, which they had liberated, on August 31, 1919 (the "Kyiv catastrophe"), leading to the collapse of the front[2].

April 1920
T0036 Installation of a New System of Governance
C1176 Sovietization of Chechnya: revkoms in place of Soviets, punitive operations and Cheka executions (1920-1924)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

Soviet Russia placed an unelected extraordinary body over Chechnya: on 3 April 1920 a congress in Grozny "proclaimed the transfer of all power to the Chechen Revolutionary Committee of five members"[9]. When at the elections to the Soviets in July 1920 "the overwhelming majority of seats in the Soviets went to... representatives of the so-called 'kulak-mullah and nationalist elements'", the elected Soviets were dissolved - historians Ya. Z. Akhmadov and E. Kh. Khasmagomadov record: "within a short span the peasant Soviets were dissolved twice (in August 1921 and in July 1922) and the power of the revkom was restored again"[9]. In the autumn of 1920 "the Aldy congress was dispersed, and several Grozny party workers... arrested" on the orders of Ordzhonikidze, head of the Caucasus Bureau of the Communist Party's Central Committee[9]. The Mountain ASSR, which had given legal form to the highlanders' autonomy, "did not last long and in 1924, having fulfilled its tasks as conceived by the party leadership, ceased to exist"[9].

April 1920
T0155 Forced Disarmament
C1176 Sovietization of Chechnya: revkoms in place of Soviets, punitive operations and Cheka executions (1920-1924)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0010 Regular Army

Soviet Russia disarmed Chechen villages by military operations through all five years of the campaign. Historian E. F. Zhupikova writes: the Caucasus Labor Army "conducted" disarmament operations "...in 1920; in March-August 1921 the Chechen auls of Gordali, Tsentoroy, Belgatoy, Dargo, Benoy, Vedeno, Groznensky and Shali, which had taken part in the mutiny of N. Gotsinsky, were disarmed"[10]. In May 1922 the troops sent into Chechnya were headed by Gikalo - secretary of the Mountain oblast committee (obkom) of the party[11], who in the war with Denikin had commanded insurgent detachments in the mountains of Chechnya[10]: "The villages of Makhkety, Goity and Katyr-Yurt were subjected to disarmament, with combat aviation used against the latter. Besides the seizure of a significant quantity of weapons, up to 30 'bandits' were captured and shot, and their property was confiscated by the authorities"[9]. The year 1924 "ended in the Chechen Autonomous Oblast with a large military operation to seize weapons, in the course of which almost 3 thousand rifles and about 400 revolvers and pistols were collected"[9].

April 1920
T0022 Terror
C1176 Sovietization of Chechnya: revkoms in place of Soviets, punitive operations and Cheka executions (1920-1924)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

Soviet Russia carried out executions for show: "During the Chechen congress in Grozny, by verdict of the local Cheka, 7 Chechens were demonstratively shot. The execution made such '...a devastating impression on the masses of Chechens who had not yet had time to disperse after the congress' that the command of the Caucasus Labor Army demanded that henceforth death sentences on Chechens be carried out as far away from Chechnya as possible"[9].

April 1920
T0021 Neutralization of the Opposition
C1176 Sovietization of Chechnya: revkoms in place of Soviets, punitive operations and Cheka executions (1920-1924)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

Over Sheikh Ali Mitaev, a member of the Chechen revkom, the GPU set "thorough surveillance", and in the spring of 1924 it "took the decision 'on the removal' of A. Mitaev from Chechnya. The official agreements with him were treacherously broken; he was suddenly arrested, taken to Rostov-on-Don and, after a brief investigation, shot"[9]; historian E. F. Zhupikova dates the arrest to 19 April 1924 and cites the GPU's formula of accusation - "for his connection with counter-revolutionary organizations"[10]. The historians call the character of the reprisal and its perpetrators by their proper names: "The murder of A. Mitaev by the Chekists," write Ya. Z. Akhmadov and E. Kh. Khasmagomadov[9].

April 1920
T0099 Hostage-Taking
C1176 Sovietization of Chechnya: revkoms in place of Soviets, punitive operations and Cheka executions (1920-1924)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

In 1923-1924 the Chechen revkom made hostage-taking an official practice of the fight against "banditry": "Measures were taken to register all criminals present in Chechnya, and the practice was introduced of taking hostages from among the close relatives of bandits in hiding"[9].

April 1920
T0028 False Compromise / Entrapment
C1176 Sovietization of Chechnya: revkoms in place of Soviets, punitive operations and Cheka executions (1920-1924)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0012 Occupation and Controlled Administrations

Soviet Russia gave the sharia courts official status and took it away within a month: the constituent congress of Soviets of the Mountain Republic in April 1921 resolved to introduce a sharia court system (sharia - Islamic law) - historians Ya. Z. Akhmadov and E. Kh. Khasmagomadov call this "a clear (and temporary) concession to the Muslim clergy of Chechnya and Ingushetia": "Already in May 1921 the TsIK of the GASSR by its resolution deprived the sharia courts of financing from the state budget and of the support of state bodies"[9].

April 1920
T0164 Pillage
C1176 Sovietization of Chechnya: revkoms in place of Soviets, punitive operations and Cheka executions (1920-1924)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0010 Regular Army

In 1920 the Red Army looted the Chechen villages where it was stationed: the units quartered in Chechnya, historians Ya. Z. Akhmadov and E. Kh. Khasmagomadov write, "received almost no property or food allowance", which pushed them into "self-supply" - "...the Red Army not only ran riot, but took away grain, cattle and so forth, terrorizing the population with senseless killings"[9]. In the summer of 1920, in Tsentoroy, the Chechens routed a unit of the Caucasus Labor Army "engaged in outright plunder"[9]. The property of the killed was taken too: during the disarmament of May 1922 "up to 30 'bandits' were captured and shot, and their property was confiscated by the authorities"[9].

April 1920
T0162.003 Killing of Prisoners
C1176 Sovietization of Chechnya: revkoms in place of Soviets, punitive operations and Cheka executions (1920-1924)
G0013 Soviet Russia (RSFSR)
S0017 Secret Police and Security Services

The special department of the Caucasus Labor Army carried out mass shootings uncontrolled even by its own command: army commissar Vrachev complained that of them "...almost nothing is known not only to the Revolutionary Tribunal or the Political Department of the army, but to me or the army commander either"[9]. During the disarmament of May 1922 people were first captured and then shot: "up to 30 'bandits' were captured and shot, and their property was confiscated by the authorities"[9].
